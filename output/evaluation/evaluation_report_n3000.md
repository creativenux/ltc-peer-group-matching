Across 20 replicate datasets of 3,000 synthetic profiles, the weighted Gower method produced a mean silhouette of 0.133 on the weighted Gower matrix, against -0.294 for random assignment, -0.244 for age-band matching and -0.232 for support-goal matching. The unmatched rate was identical for every method (1.7%), because all methods share the same pools and group-size plan. Silhouette values are low in absolute terms because each pool is divided into many groups of six to eight drawn from people who share many attribute values, so neighbouring groups are similar; the comparison between methods is what the metric is used for here.

### For the dissertation

The hypothesis is supported on the primary metric: the weighted method's silhouette on the weighted Gower matrix was significantly higher than 3 of the 3 baselines named in the hypothesis (random assignment and both single-attribute methods) after Holm correction. On the unweighted matrix, which the weighted method does not optimise, it was significantly higher than 3 of the 3, so the advantage does not depend on the distance used to measure it. The comparison with unweighted Gower is different: each Gower variant achieved the higher silhouette on the distance it optimises (weighted 0.133 vs 0.056 on the weighted matrix; unweighted 0.137 vs 0.102 on the unweighted matrix), so silhouette cannot rank the two. What the weights change is what group members share: within-group dissimilarity was lower under the weighted method for condition sub-type 0.019 vs 0.044, support goal 0.076 vs 0.176, age band 0.161 vs 0.206, time lived with the condition 0.207 vs 0.226, and lower under the unweighted method for gender 0.066 vs 0.187, engagement level 0.156 vs 0.280.

### For the dissertation

Each of the eight weights was changed by plus and minus 10% (with the others rescaled to sum to 1) and the weighted method re-run on the same dataset (seed 42). Every change altered a substantial share of group memberships (ARI 0.452 to 0.704), comparable to re-running the unchanged method with a different random seed (ARI 0.373). This reflects many near-equivalent groupings: many profiles share the same attribute values, so different memberships can be equally cohesive. Group quality was stable: the mean within-group distance, measured under the original weights, ranged from 0.1117 to 0.1165 across the perturbations, against 0.1130 for the original grouping, 0.1125 for the different seed and 0.4347 for random assignment (mean across replicates). Changes to the condition sub-type and support goal weights disturbed membership least (mean change 0.328 and 0.459); the other six differ from one another by at most 0.021, too little to rank them.

## Limitations

- The support goal, support orientation, engagement level and isolation score are synthetic, based on stated assumptions rather than population data. The results show how the method behaves on data built to those assumptions, not how real groups would form.
- The evaluation measures group similarity only. Whether cohesive groups improve wellbeing or reduce loneliness is outside the scope of this project.
- The weights are derived from the literature, not fitted to outcome data; section 3 tests how far the groupings depend on them.
- The cohesion index is specific to this project and not externally validated. Silhouette is the established measure.
- Silhouette on the weighted Gower matrix favours the weighted method, because that method optimises this distance; the unweighted silhouette is reported as a check.
- The shared-language rule leaves people in very small language pools unmatched; the unmatched rate reports this.
